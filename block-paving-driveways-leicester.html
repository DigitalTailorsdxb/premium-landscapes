Block Paving Patterns & Styles

Herringbone

The most popular pattern for driveways — interlocking at 45° or 90°, herringbone distributes vehicle load evenly across the surface and is the most structurally stable pattern. Classic look, suits all property types.

Basket Weave

A traditional square pattern that suits period and cottage-style homes. Pairs of blocks alternate direction to create a woven effect. Slightly less structural than herringbone but visually distinctive.

Stretcher Bond

A clean, contemporary running bond pattern — similar to brickwork — that suits modern properties. Ideal for combining with porcelain or resin bound sections for a premium finish.

Random / Mixed Pattern

Combining block sizes and colours for a more natural, textured appearance. Popular for larger driveways where visual interest is as important as function.

Our Block Paving Installation Process

Step 1 — Excavation: Existing surface and topsoil removed to 200–250mm depth. All waste taken from site.

Step 2 — Sub-base: Compacted MOT Type 1 hardcore laid at 150mm depth — the most important part of any block paving job. Get this wrong and the surface will sink, spread or crack within a few years.

Step 3 — Edge Restraints: Concrete haunching is laid along all perimeter edges before any blocks go down. This stops the surface spreading over time, which is the most common cause of block paving failure.

Step 4 — Sand Bed: A 25–40mm screed of sharp sand is laid and levelled as a bed for the blocks.

Step 5 — Block Laying: Blocks are laid to your chosen pattern, cut precisely at edges and perimeters. Each row is checked for level as it progresses.

Step 6 — Compaction & Jointing: The surface is compacted with a plate compactor, kiln-dried sand is brushed into the joints and the surface is compacted again. All waste and surplus material removed before handover.

Why Block Paving Is Ideal for Leicester Driveways

Leicestershire's clay-heavy ground conditions make proper sub-base preparation especially critical. Block paving installed over a compacted hardcore sub-base is one of the most resilient surfaces available in the UK climate — it handles vehicle loading, freeze-thaw cycles and the wet Midlands winters without cracking or lifting. And because individual blocks can be lifted and relaid, any future utility work under the driveway doesn't require a full surface replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most standard driveways take 3–5 working days from excavation to completion. Larger or more complex jobs may take up to 7–10 days.
Block paving with permeable jointing sand does not require planning permission for front drives. Impermeable surfaces over 5m² require planning permission under UK regulations — we advise on this at survey stage.
Proper installation with quality kiln-dried jointing sand and a polymeric sealant significantly reduces weed growth. We recommend re-sanding and sealing every 3–5 years as part of routine maintenance.
We carry a wide range of block paving products and can often closely match existing surfaces for extensions. Bring us a sample block and we'll advise on the best match available.
Properly installed block paving on a correct sub-base should last 20–30 years with normal maintenance. Individual damaged blocks can be replaced without affecting the rest of the surface.
