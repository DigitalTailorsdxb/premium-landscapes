Does a New Patio Add Value to Your House in Leicester?

By Lewis Reid · 6 years landscaping experience | 21 July 2026 | Garden Design

It's a question that comes up on a significant proportion of our quoting calls, particularly from homeowners who are thinking about selling in the next 1–3 years: is a new patio actually worth it from a property value perspective, or are we spending money that we won't get back?

The honest answer is: it depends on what you're starting from, what the rest of the house looks like, and how long you're planning to stay. Here's the full picture for Leicester specifically.

What UK Research Says About Garden Value

Rightmove data consistently shows that gardens are among the top three most searched-for features by homebuyers in the UK, alongside parking and proximity to schools.

A 2023 Savills survey found that well-maintained outdoor space added between 5% and 15% to sale price compared to equivalent properties with neglected gardens.

Halifax research found that a landscaped garden adds an average of £25,000 to property value nationally — though this varies enormously by property type and location.

For Leicester specifically, the LE2, LE3, and LE18 markets — where properties are frequently compared against close equivalents on similar streets — the condition of the garden can be a meaningful differentiator. In a market where two three-bed semis on the same road are both listed, the one with a usable, presentable garden moves faster.

What Estate Agents in Leicester Actually Say

The consistent feedback from Leicester estate agents is that gardens add value in two ways: directly (buyers will pay more) and indirectly (properties with good outdoor space sell faster and with fewer abortive sales).

The indirect value is often more significant than the direct price premium. A property that sits on the market for 8 weeks and sells for £5,000 below asking will typically net you less than a property that sells in 3 weeks at asking price — even if the second property cost £3,000 in garden improvements to achieve that outcome.

Gardens that deter buyers

  • Overgrown or completely neglected — signals work, puts buyers off immediately
  • Poor drainage — a waterlogged lawn at a viewing is a red flag for the whole property
  • Concrete slab from the 1970s with weeds growing through — unattractive and raises questions about what else needs doing

Gardens that attract buyers

  • Clean, well-defined outdoor space that's immediately usable
  • Low-maintenance surface (artificial grass, good paving) — buyers increasingly want low maintenance
  • Off-road parking where the street permits it

Which Garden Improvements Return the Most?

Not all garden investment is equal for property value. In Leicester, the improvements with the best return-on-investment are:

1.

Off-road parking (driveway)

In LE postcodes with residential parking pressure — LE1, LE2, LE3, LE4 — a properly built driveway adds more value than almost any other single improvement. Leicester City Council's parking permit zones have increased demand for off-road parking significantly in recent years.

2.

A clean, usable patio

A new porcelain or natural stone patio that's immediately usable adds to perceived property value and photographs well for listings. It doesn't need to be large — a well-defined 20–30m² patio is more valuable than a large but tired existing one.

3.

Artificial grass or a well-maintained lawn

A green, usable lawn area photographs well and feels low-maintenance to buyers. Natural turf needs to be in good condition for viewings — if your lawn is a mud patch, artificial grass before listing is worth serious consideration.

4.

Fencing and boundaries

Tired, leaning, or broken fencing is an immediate detractor at viewings. New fence panels cost relatively little and make a significant visual difference.

The Timing Question

If you're planning to sell within 12 months, the calculation changes. You won't get the enjoyment value — you're purely investing in sale price and speed. In this scenario, focus on presentation over transformation. A fresh patio or artificial grass in good condition is a better investment than a full garden makeover you won't enjoy. Clean, tidy, and photogenic is the goal.

If you're staying for 3–5 years, the investment case is stronger. You get the enjoyment benefit during your remaining time in the property, and the improvement will have bedded in properly (plants established, patios settled) by the time you sell.

A Practical Example

A Leicester semi-detached with a neglected rear garden — concrete slab, dead lawn, leaning fences — lists at £280,000. A comparable property on the same street with a new porcelain patio, artificial grass, and new fencing lists at £285,000. The second property sells in 2 weeks; the first takes 8 weeks and sells at £277,000 after two price reductions.

The garden improvement cost the second homeowner £6,500. The difference in net proceeds is approximately £8,000 — not counting the time, stress, and carrying costs of an 8-week additional marketing period.

This is a realistic scenario in the current Leicester market, not a best-case one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a new patio increase house value in Leicester?

A clean, well-installed patio can add 5–10% to perceived property value and, more importantly, helps properties sell faster. In Leicester's competitive semi-detached market, outdoor space condition is a meaningful differentiator between comparable properties.

What garden improvements add the most value in Leicester?

Off-road parking (driveway) adds the most in LE postcodes with parking pressure. Close behind: a clean usable patio, artificial grass or a well-maintained lawn, and new fencing. These are all visible at viewings and photograph well for listings.

Is it worth landscaping a garden before selling in Leicester?

Usually yes if the garden is currently a detractor — waterlogged, overgrown, or dominated by broken concrete. The cost of basic improvement (patio or artificial grass) typically pays back in faster sale and fewer price reductions. Full makeovers are harder to justify purely on ROI unless you're staying for a few more years.

How long before selling should I landscape my garden?

Ideally 2–6 months before listing. This gives new turf time to establish, gives any new paving time to settle and weather slightly (brand-new paving can look harsh in photos), and gives you time to address any issues before viewings start.

Do Leicester estate agents recommend garden improvements before listing?

Generally yes for properties where the garden is noticeably poor compared to local comparables. For properties where the garden is already average or above-average, the return on additional investment is lower. We're happy to give a pre-sale landscaping assessment alongside a quote.
