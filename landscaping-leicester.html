Leicester Site Considerations

Leicester gardens range from compact terraces with restricted access to larger suburban plots. A useful first brief includes the garden's intended use, any change in levels, access for materials and whether water currently collects in a particular area.

Drainage and ground preparation should be assessed for the individual site before paving, turf or decking is specified. If your property is in a conservation area, listed, or has a front-garden surfacing proposal, check the current requirements with Leicester City Council.

Planning Permission & Leicester

Local Planning Authority: Leicester City Council

Responsible for planning decisions in Leicester and the surrounding LE1–LE5 area.

Leicester City Council is the local planning authority. Most domestic patio, artificial grass and decking projects fall under permitted development. The New Walk and Victoria Park Conservation Areas impose stricter rules — any work on properties within those boundaries, or fronting the highway, should be checked before installation. We advise on planning requirements at survey stage.

What typically does not need planning permission in Leicester

  • Rear garden patio and hard landscaping at ground level
  • Artificial grass in rear or front gardens (replacing natural grass)
  • Composite or timber decking up to 30cm above ground level and not more than 50% of the rear garden
  • Garden lighting using low-voltage (12V) transformers
  • Rear garden fencing up to 2 metres high

What may require planning consent in Leicester

  • Decking over 30cm high or covering more than 50% of the rear garden
  • Front garden hard-surfacing over 5m² replacing grass (must use permeable surface or obtain permission)
  • Work in a designated conservation area or on a listed building
  • Large outbuildings or pergolas exceeding the permitted development size limits

Planning rules can change and are subject to local variations. We provide planning guidance at no charge during our survey visit — if in doubt, always verify with Leicester City Council directly before proceeding.
