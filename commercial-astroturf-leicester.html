Why Commercial Astroturf Is Different from Domestic

Commercial-grade synthetic surfaces are engineered for hundreds of foot traffic events per day — not the dozen or so a domestic garden sees. This means thicker pile densities, more aggressive thatch backing, UV-stabilisers rated to 15-20 years and infill systems that maintain pile recovery under heavy use.

Safety compliance is the other major difference. School and nursery installations must meet specific Critical Fall Height (CFH) ratings under EN 1177 for the play equipment they surround — meaning the right shock pad thickness for each piece of equipment. We design, document and certify CFH compliance as part of every play installation.

Project management on commercial sites also differs: full Construction Phase Plans, RAMS documentation, site inductions, hoarding, parent/staff communication, term-time scheduling, hourly working windows and out-of-hours installation are all standard for our school and nursery work.

Our Commercial Installation Process

Step 1 — Initial Consultation & Site Survey

Free site visit to discuss use case, traffic levels, drainage, access and any site-specific constraints (school hours, security, neighbour impact). For larger projects we provide a detailed written specification and CAD layout.

Step 2 — Specification & Pricing

Full written quotation with system specification (pile height, infill, shock pad CFH ratings if applicable), drainage strategy, base build-up, edging details and project timeline. Itemised so you can value-engineer if needed.

Step 3 — Documentation & Approvals

Full Construction Phase Plan, RAMS, insurance documentation and DBS certificates supplied before mobilisation. Where required, we attend pre-start meetings with your facilities manager, headteacher or project manager.

Step 4 — Installation

Site secured with hoarding/fencing as appropriate. Excavation, sub-base installation (typically 100-150mm Type 1), geotextile membrane, shock pad (for play surfaces), synthetic turf laid, seamed, edged and infilled. Inspection and sign-off.

Step 5 — Handover & Aftercare

Full as-built documentation, CFH certification (where applicable), warranty paperwork and maintenance schedule. Optional ongoing maintenance contracts available — typically annual brushing, infill top-up and inspection.

Maintenance & Aftercare Contracts

Commercial astroturf needs minimal but specific maintenance to maintain warranty and lifespan: regular surface brushing to keep the pile upright, occasional infill top-up where sand or rubber has migrated, and periodic deep cleaning to remove organic debris. We offer one-off or annual maintenance contracts — much more cost-effective than allowing the surface to degrade.
