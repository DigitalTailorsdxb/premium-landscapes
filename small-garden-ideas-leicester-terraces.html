Small Garden Landscaping Ideas for Leicester Terraces

By Lewis Reid · 6 years landscaping experience | 14 July 2026 | Garden Design

Small rear gardens are the reality for a significant proportion of Leicester homeowners. In LE1, LE2, LE3, and LE4 — the Victorian and Edwardian terraces that make up large stretches of the city — rear gardens run 5–10 metres deep and 3–5 metres wide. They're overlooked by neighbours, shaded by back-of-terrace walls, and used for everything from bin storage to outdoor eating.

Getting the design right in a space like this isn't about cramming more in — it's about making deliberate choices that give the garden a sense of purpose and scale. Here's what actually works.

Simplify the Ground Surface

The most common mistake in small Leicester terrace gardens is using too many different materials. Patches of old concrete next to tired grass next to stepping stones reads as chaotic and makes the space feel smaller.

A single continuous ground surface — porcelain patio running the full width, or artificial grass edge-to-edge — unifies the space and makes it feel larger. The eye reads it as one plane rather than multiple competing elements.

For north-facing Leicester terrace gardens (a large proportion of LE postcode rears face north or north-east), artificial grass outperforms natural turf significantly — it stays green and usable year-round rather than the mud patch that most north-facing Leicester lawns become from October to April.

For south-facing gardens, porcelain paving in a light colour (cream, beige, silver-grey) reflects available light and makes the space feel more open. Dark materials — charcoal porcelain, black granite — absorb light and make small spaces feel enclosed.

Use Vertical Space

In a small footprint, the walls and fences are as valuable as the floor. Three vertical approaches that work well in Leicester terrace gardens:

Trellis panels with climbing plants: Fix trellis above existing fencing to add 300–600mm of height, then grow Clematis, Jasmine, or climbing Roses up it. Adds privacy, softens the boundary, and draws the eye upward — making the garden feel taller.

Wall-mounted planters: Galvanised steel or powder-coated wall planters on the rear fence allow planting without losing floor space. Good for herbs, seasonal flowers, or trailing plants that soften the boundary.

Raised beds: A raised bed along one or two boundaries creates a defined planting area that doesn't eat into the central floor space. A 300mm-high brick or sleeper raised bed along the rear fence adds structure, planting depth, and a visual anchor to the back of the garden.

Define a Single Zone

Small gardens work better with one clearly defined purpose than multiple vague zones. A 5×8m garden trying to be a dining area, a lawn, a planting garden, and a storage area simultaneously is none of those things.

The most successful small Leicester terrace gardens we design are built around a single central zone — usually a patio large enough to seat 4–6 people — with planting and storage resolved at the edges. The patio becomes the room; the planting is the decoration.

For most Leicester terraces, a 3×4m or 3×5m patio area takes up the majority of the garden and leaves a 1–1.5m planted border along the rear or side boundary. This balance looks intentional, is low maintenance, and creates a genuinely usable outdoor room.

Storage: Solve It in the Design

Bin storage, bike storage, and garden tool storage in a small Leicester terrace garden should be part of the design, not an afterthought. A bin enclosure built from matching fence panels or brick, positioned next to the back gate, removes the visual clutter that undermines even well-designed small gardens.

A small timber or metal log store against the rear fence doubles as a visual feature. Purpose-built bike storage (a lean-to against the house or a freestanding unit) keeps bikes off the patio and out of sight.

Lighting Changes Everything

Small gardens in Leicester are unusable from October to March if they're not lit. Low-voltage LED lighting — integrated into the patio edge, mounted low on fence posts, or positioned within planting borders — extends the usable season significantly and transforms how the space reads in the evening.

For small terraces specifically, the best return from lighting comes from: one uplight on a tree or large shrub if present, two or three ground-level path lights along the patio edge, and a string of overhead lights between the house wall and the rear fence. The overhead string light is particularly effective in small terraces — it creates a canopy effect that defines the outdoor room.

What to Avoid in Small Leicester Gardens

Decking with balustrades

A raised deck in a small space adds visual complexity and height restrictions that work against the sense of openness. Ground-level paving almost always looks better in a small terrace.

Lots of small pots

Individual pots dotted around a small garden look cluttered. One or two large statement containers work far better.

A square of grass

A small lawn in a shaded Leicester terrace will be a mud patch from October onwards. If you want green, use artificial grass on a proper sub-base.

Cheap fencing

In a small garden, the fences are always visible. A tired, leaning fence panel undermines everything else. We rebuild boundaries as part of most small garden makeover projects in Leicester because it makes that much difference.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to landscape a small terraced garden in Leicester?

A typical small Leicester terrace makeover — patio, artificial grass or borders, new fence, lighting — runs £4,000–£9,000 depending on materials and scope. Smaller scope projects (patio only, or artificial grass only) start from £1,500–£2,500.

What's the best patio material for a small north-facing Leicester garden?

Light-coloured porcelain or natural stone in a pale grey, cream, or buff tone reflects available daylight and will make the space feel less enclosed. Avoid dark materials in north-facing gardens.

Can artificial grass work in a very small Leicester garden?

Yes — it's often the best option in small shaded terrace gardens where natural turf would struggle. Even a 2×3m strip of quality artificial grass on a proper sub-base stays looking good year-round.

How do I make a small Leicester garden feel bigger?

Single continuous ground surface, light-coloured materials, vertical planting on boundaries, minimal furniture, strategic lighting, and resolved storage. Simplicity reads as space.

Do I need planning permission to landscape my terrace garden in Leicester?

Not for standard rear garden landscaping — patios, artificial grass, fencing up to 2m, raised beds. Structures (sheds, large pergolas) may have limits under permitted development. We advise at the quoting stage.
